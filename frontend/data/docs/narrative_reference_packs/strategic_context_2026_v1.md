## When To Use

Use this pack for participant-facing Scenario Reviews when the narrative should raise expert strategic questions about:

* decentralized or hybrid trial elements
* participant access, diversity, representativeness, and generalizability
* quality by design and critical-to-quality factors
* estimand and endpoint interpretability
* operational proportionality
* digital, remote, or AI-enabled data collection and oversight risks
* whether a design shortcut improves completion outlook at the expense of decision quality

## Key Principles

### 1. Completion favorability is not the same as strategic quality

A trial can become easier to complete while becoming less capable of answering the development question.

Narratives should distinguish operational simplification from stronger evidence generation.

### 2. Access, representativeness, and feasibility are strategic design issues

Modern trial strategy increasingly treats enrollment diversity, local access, decentralised elements, participant burden, and retention as design-quality concerns rather than purely operational details.

The narrative should ask whether the scenario improves the relevance and feasibility of evidence without weakening interpretability.

### 3. Decentralized or digital elements require data-quality and oversight reasoning

Remote visits, local-care activities, home health, digital endpoints, and wearable data may improve access and retention but can introduce measurement variability, missingness, monitoring complexity, privacy concerns, and site/vendor oversight needs.

The narrative should treat decentralization as a trade-off, not as automatically modern or automatically weak.

### 4. Estimand and endpoint clarity matter more as designs become more flexible

Adaptive, pragmatic, remote, or complex designs should still make the treatment condition, population, endpoint, intercurrent-event handling, and interpretation clear enough to support the intended decision.

### 5. Questions should elevate the discussion

Participant questions should not ask whether a field should be changed. They should ask what evidence standard, governance burden, population trade-off, or operational rationale would make the scenario strategically defensible.

## Relevance To Simulator Pillars

### Phase & Intent Alignment

Use this pack to ask whether the scenario fits the development decision it appears to claim, especially when completion favorability improves through lower ambition or weaker evidence requirements.

### Endpoint & Evidence Strength

Use this pack to evaluate whether endpoint, comparator, duration, decentralised measurement, and analysis assumptions can support interpretable evidence.

### Target Population Alignment

Use this pack to evaluate whether eligibility, biomarker selection, demographics, disease severity, access, and burden create a patient population that is relevant to the intended use.

### Operational Burden Balance

Use this pack to evaluate whether enrollment, sites, follow-up, decentralised activities, digital collection, monitoring, and governance are proportionate to the evidence ambition.

## Do Not Infer

Do not infer that:

* decentralized design is always better or always weaker
* diversity, broad eligibility, or access improvements automatically improve interpretability
* digital or remote data collection is reliable without oversight and missing-data reasoning
* operational simplification is strategically superior just because Completion Outlook improves
* current strategic context overrides the scenario packet
* the trial is compliant or non-compliant with a regulation
* regulators would accept or reject the design

## Prompt-Safe Summary

Current clinical-trial strategy increasingly treats quality by design, participant access, representativeness, decentralised elements, data reliability, estimand clarity, and risk-proportionate oversight as connected design issues. A scenario can improve Completion Outlook by simplifying execution while weakening endpoint interpretability, comparator credibility, population relevance, or governance. Conversely, a harder-to-complete scenario may be more strategically defensible if the added burden supports a clearer development decision, more relevant population, stronger endpoint maturity, or better oversight. Use this pack to raise open strategic questions about evidence standards, population trade-offs, operational proportionality, and governance burden. Do not treat modern practices such as decentralization, digital collection, or broader access as automatically positive; judge whether they are supported by the scenario packet and whether they improve decision quality.
