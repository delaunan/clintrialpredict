## When To Use

Use this pack when scenario changes affect:

* participant protection
* data integrity
* sponsor oversight
* site and investigator burden
* feasibility of trial conduct
* risk-proportionate quality management
* monitoring and quality control
* protocol adherence
* safety monitoring
* operational controls
* use of service providers
* decentralised or complex trial execution
* reliability of trial results

This pack should support operational and quality reasoning. It should not turn the CTPredict narrative into a compliance audit.

## Key Principles

### 1. GCP protects participants and supports reliable results

Good Clinical Practice is centered on protecting trial participants and ensuring that trial data and results are reliable.

For CTPredict narratives, this means that operational design choices should be interpreted through two questions:

* Could this choice affect participant rights, safety, or well-being?
* Could this choice affect the reliability or interpretability of trial results?

A design is stronger when operational choices support both aims.

### 2. Quality management should be proportionate to risk

E6(R3) supports a proportionate and risk-based approach to quality management.

This means that not every operational risk deserves the same level of control. The strongest designs identify the risks that matter most and manage those risks without creating unnecessary procedural burden.

For CTPredict, this is useful when evaluating whether the trial has become too complex, too light, or appropriately controlled for its phase and objective.

### 3. Sponsor oversight remains important even when work is delegated

Delegating activities to service providers does not remove the need for sponsor oversight.

For narratives, this matters when a design includes many sites, complex procedures, decentralised elements, external vendors, central laboratories, imaging readers, adjudication committees, or digital systems.

The issue is not simply whether these elements exist. The issue is whether the resulting operational model remains credible and controllable.

### 4. Investigator and site capability are part of feasibility

A trial design is stronger when the required sites and investigators can realistically perform the protocol.

Site capability includes:

* appropriate qualifications and training
* sufficient resources and facilities
* ability to recruit the target population
* ability to perform trial-specific procedures
* ability to collect reliable data
* ability to manage safety and investigational product requirements
* ability to follow the protocol

A design may be scientifically attractive but operationally weak if site capability is unlikely to match protocol complexity.

### 5. Data integrity is part of design coherence

Reliable trial results depend on data that can be collected, traced, reviewed, protected, corrected, transferred, finalised, retained, and interpreted.

The narrative should treat data collection burden and data governance as design issues, not merely technical issues.

A design may be weaker if it requires too much data, depends on fragile systems, creates unclear source-data expectations, or increases the risk of missing, inconsistent, or poorly interpretable data.

### 6. Monitoring should focus on what matters most

Monitoring is not only about checking everything. It should focus on participant protection and reliability of trial results.

The narrative should be stronger when it identifies whether the design creates monitoring needs that are proportionate to:

* trial complexity
* participant risk
* endpoint criticality
* site number and variability
* data criticality
* decentralised or vendor-based activities
* blinding and randomisation risks

More monitoring is not automatically better. The issue is whether oversight is targeted to the critical risks.

### 7. Protocol adherence matters when deviations affect interpretation

Protocol deviations are most important when they affect participant safety, data completeness, data accuracy, or reliability of results.

The narrative should not overreact to every operational imperfection. It should focus on deviations or feasibility risks that could prevent the trial from answering its intended question.

### 8. Safety monitoring must fit the development stage and risk profile

Safety monitoring should be proportionate to the known and potential risks of the investigational product, the population, the phase, the treatment duration, and the endpoint strategy.

The narrative should consider whether operational assumptions create enough capacity to detect, report, and manage safety issues.

This is especially relevant in fragile populations, high-risk interventions, long follow-up, first-in-human trials, complex dosing, or trials with decentralised elements.

### 9. Operational complexity can be justified

Complexity is not automatically negative.

Additional sites, vendors, committees, data systems, procedures, or monitoring layers may be justified when they protect participants, improve data reliability, reduce bias, or strengthen endpoint interpretation.

Complexity is weaker when it increases burden without improving the trial’s ability to answer the clinical question.

### 10. GCP should not be used as a generic penalty

The narrative should not penalise a design simply because it is ambitious, decentralised, multi-site, adaptive, or operationally complex.

The correct question is whether the operational model is proportionate, controlled, and credible for the trial objective.

## Relevance To Simulator Pillars

### Phase & Intent Alignment

Use E6(R3) to assess whether the operational controls fit the trial phase and purpose.

Early exploratory trials may need flexibility, but they still need participant protection and reliable data. Later confirmatory trials usually need stronger oversight, endpoint reliability, and protocol adherence.

### Endpoint & Evidence Strength

Use E6(R3) to assess whether the endpoint data can realistically be collected, protected, monitored, and interpreted.

Endpoint strength depends not only on what is measured, but also on whether the data collection process is reliable.

### Target Population Alignment

Use E6(R3) to assess whether the operational model fits the population.

A population may be difficult to recruit, fragile, geographically dispersed, treatment-experienced, or dependent on specialist sites. These factors affect feasibility, burden, consent, retention, safety monitoring, and data quality.

### Operational Burden Balance

Use E6(R3) to assess whether enrollment, site count, procedures, monitoring, vendors, safety reporting, and data governance are proportionate to the evidence value.

Operational burden should be interpreted as a trade-off. It can be acceptable when justified by safety or evidence quality.

### Design Coherence

Use E6(R3) to assess whether the trial can be executed in a way that preserves participant protection and reliability of results.

A coherent design aligns protocol complexity, site capability, oversight model, data collection, monitoring, safety management, and expected decision use.

## Do Not Infer

Do not infer that:

* the design is compliant or non-compliant
* regulators would accept or reject the design
* more monitoring is always better
* fewer sites are always better
* more sites are always better
* every service provider increases risk
* every decentralised element is risky
* every operational burden is negative
* every protocol deviation invalidates the trial
* GCP requires maximal procedural complexity
* exploratory trials require the same operational model as confirmatory trials

Do not use this pack to provide regulatory advice, legal advice, medical advice, or a compliance determination.

## Prompt-Safe Summary

ICH E6(R3) supports CTPredict narratives when design changes affect trial conduct, participant protection, data integrity, oversight, monitoring, site burden, or operational feasibility. The main question is whether the operational model is proportionate to the trial’s risks, complexity, phase, and objective. A stronger design protects participant rights, safety, and well-being while preserving reliable and interpretable trial results. Sponsor oversight, investigator and site capability, data governance, safety monitoring, and quality control should be credible for the trial design. Complexity is not automatically negative: additional sites, vendors, committees, systems, or monitoring may be justified if they protect participants, reduce bias, improve data reliability, or strengthen endpoint interpretation. Complexity is weaker when it adds burden without improving the trial’s ability to answer its intended question. Use this pack for practical, conditional feedback on operational credibility and risk-proportionate conduct, not for compliance judgments.
