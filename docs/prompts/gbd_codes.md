# **IHME GBD 2021: Hierarchical Indication Menu (v02)**
Use this list to map trials to the most granular ID possible.
**Logic**: Find L4 match first -> Fallback to L3 -> Fallback to the Group's [L2 Safety Net] ID -> Final Fallback [ID: 0].

### **GROUP: HIV/AIDS and sexually transmitted infections**
**[Safety Net] [L2] [ID: 955] HIV/AIDS and sexually transmitted infections** | Infections primarily transmitted through sexual contact or blood, including HIV/AIDS and other STIs.

- [L3] [ID: 298] HIV/AIDS | Infection by Human Immunodeficiency Virus leading to Progressive immune failure and opportunistic infections.
  - [L4] [ID: 948] HIV/AIDS - Drug-susceptible Tuberculosis | Tuberculosis infection in a patient already suffering from HIV/AIDS.
  - [L4] [ID: 949] HIV/AIDS - Multidrug-resistant Tuberculosis without extensive drug resistance | MDR-TB infection in a patient already suffering from HIV/AIDS.
  - [L4] [ID: 950] HIV/AIDS - Extensively drug-resistant Tuberculosis | XDR-TB infection in a patient already suffering from HIV/AIDS.
  - [L4] [ID: 300] HIV/AIDS resulting in other diseases | Systemic complications and secondary pathologies resulting directly from HIV/AIDS progression.
- [L3] [ID: 393] Sexually transmitted infections excluding HIV | Bacterial, viral, and parasitic infections primarily spread through sexual contact, excluding HIV.
  - [L4] [ID: 394] Syphilis | Chronic bacterial infection caused by Treponema pallidum, progressing through clinical stages.
  - [L4] [ID: 395] Chlamydial infection | Common STI caused by Chlamydia trachomatis, often asymptomatic but causing PID if untreated.
  - [L4] [ID: 396] Gonococcal infection | Bacterial infection caused by Neisseria gonorrhoeae, affecting mucous membranes.
  - [L4] [ID: 397] Trichomoniasis | Parasitic infection caused by Trichomonas vaginalis, causing urogenital inflammation.
  - [L4] [ID: 398] Genital herpes | Viral infection caused by HSV-1 or HSV-2, characterized by recurrent genital lesions.
  - [L4] [ID: 399] Other sexually transmitted infections | Other STIs including chancroid, granuloma inguinale, and viral warts.
### **GROUP: Respiratory infections and tuberculosis**
**[Safety Net] [L2] [ID: 956] Respiratory infections and tuberculosis** | Infections affecting the respiratory tract, including tuberculosis, pneumonia, and influenza.

- [L3] [ID: 297] Tuberculosis | Infection caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis, primarily affecting lungs but also extrapulmonary sites.
  - [L4] [ID: 954] Latent tuberculosis infection | State of having the TB bacteria in the body without active symptoms.
  - [L4] [ID: 934] Drug-susceptible tuberculosis | Forms of tuberculosis that can be treated with standard first-line antibiotics.
  - [L4] [ID: 946] Multidrug-resistant tuberculosis without extensive drug resistance | TB resistant to isoniazid and rifampin, requiring specialized treatment.
  - [L4] [ID: 947] Extensively drug-resistant tuberculosis | TB resistant to first-line and several second-line drugs, extremely difficult to treat.
- [L3] [ID: 322] Lower respiratory infections | Infections of the lungs and lower airways including pneumonia, bronchitis, and bronchiolitis.
- [L3] [ID: 328] Upper respiratory infections | Acute infections of the nose, throat, and upper airways including the common cold and pharyngitis.
- [L3] [ID: 329] Otitis media | Inflammation or infection of the middle ear, often following a respiratory tract infection.
- [L3] [ID: 1048] COVID-19 | Respiratory illness caused by the SARS-CoV-2 virus.
### **GROUP: Enteric infections**
**[Safety Net] [L2] [ID: 957] Enteric infections** | Infections of the gastrointestinal tract causing diarrhea and other enteric symptoms.

- [L3] [ID: 302] Diarrheal diseases | Acute or chronic intestinal infections characterized by frequent loose stools, including Rotavirus, Norovirus, and viral gastroenteritis.
- [L3] [ID: 958] Typhoid and paratyphoid | Infections caused by Salmonella Typhi or Paratyphi bacteria.
  - [L4] [ID: 319] Typhoid fever | Severe systemic illness caused by Salmonella Typhi bacteria, often transmitted via contaminated food/water.
  - [L4] [ID: 320] Paratyphoid fever | Febrile illness caused by Salmonella Paratyphi, similar to but often milder than typhoid fever.
- [L3] [ID: 959] Invasive Non-typhoidal Salmonella (iNTS) | Severe systemic infection caused by non-typhoidal Salmonella strains.
- [L3] [ID: 321] Other intestinal infectious diseases | Other enteric infections including cholera, giardiasis, and foodborne bacterial pathogens not listed elsewhere.
### **GROUP: Neglected tropical diseases and malaria**
**[Safety Net] [L2] [ID: 344] Neglected tropical diseases and malaria** | Group of tropical diseases including malaria, Chagas, and other parasitic infections often affecting developing regions.

- [L3] [ID: 345] Malaria | Mosquito-borne infectious disease caused by Plasmodium parasites, causing cyclic fever and anemia.
- [L3] [ID: 346] Chagas disease | Tropical parasitic disease caused by Trypanosoma cruzi, often leading to heart and digestive complications.
- [L3] [ID: 347] Leishmaniasis | Disease caused by Leishmania parasites, transmitted by sandflies, affecting skin or internal organs.
  - [L4] [ID: 348] Visceral leishmaniasis | Most severe form of leishmaniasis (Kala-azar), affecting the liver, spleen, and bone marrow.
  - [L4] [ID: 349] Cutaneous and mucocutaneous leishmaniasis | Forms of leishmaniasis causing skin ulcers or destructive sores in the nose and mouth.
- [L3] [ID: 350] African trypanosomiasis | Vector-borne parasitic disease (Tsetse fly) causing neurological symptoms and sleep cycle disruption.
- [L3] [ID: 351] Schistosomiasis | Chronic disease caused by parasitic flatworms (blood flukes) affecting the liver and bladder.
- [L3] [ID: 352] Cysticercosis | Tissue infection caused by the larval stage of the pork tapeworm (Taenia solium).
- [L3] [ID: 353] Cystic echinococcosis | Parasitic disease caused by the larval stage of Echinococcus granulosus, forming cysts in organs.
- [L3] [ID: 354] Lymphatic filariasis | Parasitic infection by filarial worms causing extreme swelling of limbs (Elephantiasis).
- [L3] [ID: 355] Onchocerciasis | Parasitic infection by Onchocerca volvulus worms causing severe itching and blindness.
- [L3] [ID: 356] Trachoma | Bacterial eye infection caused by Chlamydia trachomatis, the leading preventable cause of blindness.
- [L3] [ID: 357] Dengue | Mosquito-borne viral infection (DENV) causing severe flu-like symptoms and potentially hemorrhagic fever.
- [L3] [ID: 358] Yellow fever | Acute viral hemorrhagic disease transmitted by infected mosquitoes, causing jaundice and fever.
- [L3] [ID: 359] Rabies | Viral infection of the central nervous system, almost always fatal once clinical symptoms appear.
- [L3] [ID: 360] Intestinal nematode infections | Intestinal infections caused by parasitic worms including roundworms, hookworms, and whipworms.
  - [L4] [ID: 361] Ascariasis | Intestinal infection caused by the giant roundworm Ascaris lumbricoides.
  - [L4] [ID: 362] Trichuriasis | Infection of the large intestine caused by the whipworm Trichuris trichiura.
  - [L4] [ID: 363] Hookworm disease | Intestinal parasite infection causing chronic anemia and protein malnutrition.
- [L3] [ID: 364] Food-borne trematodiases | Infections caused by liver, lung, or intestinal flukes acquired through contaminated food.
- [L3] [ID: 405] Leprosy | Chronic infectious disease caused by Mycobacterium leprae, affecting skin and peripheral nerves.
- [L3] [ID: 843] Ebola | Severe, often fatal illness caused by the Ebola virus, characterized by hemorrhagic fever.
- [L3] [ID: 935] Zika virus | Viral infection primarily spread by mosquitoes, linked to birth defects if contracted during pregnancy.
- [L3] [ID: 936] Guinea worm disease | Parasitic infection caused by the Guinea worm, acquired through contaminated water.
- [L3] [ID: 365] Other neglected tropical diseases | Diverse group of tropical infections including Buruli ulcer, yaws, and scabies not elsewhere classified.
### **GROUP: Other infectious diseases**
**[Safety Net] [L2] [ID: 961] Other infectious diseases** | Other infectious diseases not categorized in the major respiratory, enteric, or sexually transmitted groups.

- [L3] [ID: 332] Meningitis | Inflammation of the protective membranes covering the brain and spinal cord, often bacterial or viral.
- [L3] [ID: 337] Encephalitis | Acute inflammation of the brain tissue, usually resulting from a viral infection or autoimmune response.
- [L3] [ID: 338] Diphtheria | Acute bacterial infection caused by Corynebacterium diphtheriae, primarily affecting mucous membranes.
- [L3] [ID: 339] Pertussis | Highly contagious respiratory tract infection characterized by a severe hacking cough (Whooping cough).
- [L3] [ID: 340] Tetanus | Nervous system disorder caused by Clostridium tetani toxin, leading to painful muscle contractions.
- [L3] [ID: 341] Measles | Highly contagious viral disease causing fever and a characteristic red skin rash.
- [L3] [ID: 342] Varicella and herpes zoster | Infections caused by the Varicella-Zoster Virus, including chickenpox and shingles (herpes zoster).
- [L3] [ID: 400] Acute hepatitis | Acute inflammation of the liver caused by one of five hepatitis viruses (A, B, C, D, E).
  - [L4] [ID: 401] Acute hepatitis A | Acute liver infection spread through contaminated food/water (fecal-oral route).
  - [L4] [ID: 402] Acute hepatitis B | Liver infection spread via blood or bodily fluids, potentially leading to chronic disease.
  - [L4] [ID: 403] Acute hepatitis C | Blood-borne viral infection of the liver, frequently progressing to chronic cirrhosis.
  - [L4] [ID: 404] Acute hepatitis E | Hepatitis virus usually transmitted via fecal-oral route, dangerous in pregnant women.
- [L3] [ID: 408] Other unspecified infectious diseases | Infectious diseases not categorized elsewhere, including Sepsis, Septic Shock, and multi-drug resistant (MDR) bacterial infections.
### **GROUP: Maternal and neonatal disorders**
**[Safety Net] [L2] [ID: 962] Maternal and neonatal disorders** | Health conditions originating during pregnancy, childbirth, or the early neonatal period.

- [L3] [ID: 366] Maternal disorders | Health conditions resulting from or aggravated by pregnancy and childbirth.
  - [L4] [ID: 367] Maternal hemorrhage | Excessive bleeding during pregnancy, labor, or the postpartum period.
  - [L4] [ID: 368] Maternal sepsis and other maternal infections | Severe systemic infection and organ dysfunction resulting from pregnancy or delivery complications.
  - [L4] [ID: 369] Maternal hypertensive disorders | High blood pressure during pregnancy, including pre-eclampsia and eclampsia.
  - [L4] [ID: 370] Maternal obstructed labor and uterine rupture | Complications where labor is physically blocked, risking uterine rupture and fetal distress.
  - [L4] [ID: 995] Maternal abortion and miscarriage | Condition where a pregnancy ends spontaneously before the fetus is viable.
  - [L4] [ID: 374] Ectopic pregnancy | Complication where the embryo implants outside the main cavity of the uterus.
  - [L4] [ID: 375] Indirect maternal deaths | Maternal deaths resulting from previous existing disease aggravated by physiological effects of pregnancy.
  - [L4] [ID: 376] Late maternal deaths | Maternal deaths occurring more than 42 days but less than one year after delivery.
  - [L4] [ID: 741] Maternal deaths aggravated by HIV/AIDS | Maternal deaths where the underlying cause is existing HIV/AIDS.
  - [L4] [ID: 1160] Other direct maternal disorders | Other complications specifically related to pregnancy or delivery.
- [L3] [ID: 380] Neonatal disorders | Conditions and disorders originating in the first 28 days of life.
  - [L4] [ID: 381] Neonatal preterm birth | Infants born alive before 37 weeks of pregnancy are completed.
  - [L4] [ID: 382] Neonatal encephalopathy due to birth asphyxia and trauma | Brain injury in newborns caused by lack of oxygen or restricted blood flow during birth.
  - [L4] [ID: 383] Neonatal sepsis and other neonatal infections | Systemic infection in an infant less than 28 days old.
  - [L4] [ID: 384] Hemolytic disease and other neonatal jaundice | Yellowing of the skin and eyes in newborns due to high bilirubin levels or blood group incompatibility.
  - [L4] [ID: 385] Other neonatal disorders | Other conditions specific to the neonatal period including birth trauma and metabolic disturbances.

- [L3] [ID: 388] Iodine deficiency | Condition resulting from insufficient iodine intake, leading to goiter and intellectual disability.

### **GROUP: Cardiovascular diseases**
**[Safety Net] [L2] [ID: 491] Cardiovascular diseases** | Broad category for diseases of the heart and blood vessels, including stroke and ischemic heart disease.

- [L3] [ID: 492] Rheumatic heart disease | Heart damage resulting from rheumatic fever, primarily affecting the heart valves.
- [L3] [ID: 493] Ischemic heart disease | Reduced blood flow to the heart muscle due to plaque buildup in the coronary arteries.
- [L3] [ID: 494] Stroke | Sudden interruption of blood supply to the brain (ischemic) or bleeding in the brain (hemorrhagic).
  - [L4] [ID: 495] Ischemic stroke | Brain injury caused by a blocked artery (thrombus or embolus) cutting off oxygen.
  - [L4] [ID: 496] Intracerebral hemorrhage | Stroke caused by the rupture of a blood vessel within the brain tissue.
  - [L4] [ID: 497] Subarachnoid hemorrhage | Stroke caused by bleeding into the space surrounding the brain, often from an aneurysm.
- [L3] [ID: 498] Hypertensive heart disease | Heart failure or thickening of heart muscle caused by chronic high blood pressure.
- [L3] [ID: 504] Non-rheumatic valvular heart disease | Damage to one or more of the four heart valves not caused by rheumatic fever.
  - [L4] [ID: 968] Non-rheumatic calcific aortic valve disease | Hardening and narrowing of the aortic valve not caused by rheumatic disease.
  - [L4] [ID: 969] Non-rheumatic degenerative mitral valve disease | Condition where the mitral valve does not close properly due to tissue breakdown.
  - [L4] [ID: 970] Other non-rheumatic valve diseases | Other non-rheumatic disorders of the heart valves.
- [L3] [ID: 499] Cardiomyopathy and myocarditis | Diseases of the heart muscle (cardiomyopathy) or inflammation of the heart muscle (myocarditis).
  - [L4] [ID: 942] Myocarditis | Inflammation of the heart muscle, often following a viral infection.
  - [L4] [ID: 938] Alcoholic cardiomyopathy | Condition where heart muscle is weakened due to chronic excessive alcohol use.
  - [L4] [ID: 944] Other cardiomyopathy | Other diseases of the heart muscle not otherwise classified.
- [L3] [ID: 1004] Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension | High blood pressure in the arteries of the lungs, stressing the right side of the heart.
- [L3] [ID: 500] Atrial fibrillation and flutter | Irregular, often rapid heart rate that can cause poor blood flow and increase stroke risk.
- [L3] [ID: 501] Aortic aneurysm | Balloon-like bulge in the aorta, the main artery carrying blood from the heart.
- [L3] [ID: 502] Lower extremity peripheral arterial disease | Narrowing of the peripheral arteries, most commonly in the legs, leading to pain and reduced mobility.
- [L3] [ID: 503] Endocarditis | Infection of the inner lining of the heart chambers and valves (endocardium).
- [L3] [ID: 507] Other cardiovascular and circulatory diseases | Other disorders of the heart and blood vessels not otherwise classified, including Varicose Veins, Venous Insufficiency, and Peripheral Vascular Edema.

- [L3] [ID: 534] Gallbladder and biliary diseases | Inflammation or blockage of the gallbladder (cholecystitis) or bile ducts.
